Course description

This course introduces the physical processes that move water through the landscape, from precipitation and interception to infiltration, groundwater recharge and streamflow. Students learn to measure these processes in the field and to describe them with simple quantitative models. Weekly laboratory sessions use real data from the university's experimental catchment on the Ashby Brook.

The course is a prerequisite for HYD 305 Groundwater Hydraulics and for the water resources specialisation. Prior completion of MATH 120 or an equivalent course in calculus is required.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

  • describe the components of the hydrological cycle and estimate a catchment water balance;
  • analyse rainfall records and construct intensity–duration–frequency curves;
  • measure streamflow with the velocity–area method and build a stage–discharge rating curve;
  • apply the rational method and the unit hydrograph to estimate design floods;
  • write a clear technical report that states its assumptions and uncertainties.

Course policies

Attendance

Attendance at laboratory and field sessions is compulsory because the data collected there are used in the reports. Students who miss a session for a documented reason will be given an equivalent data set.

Late submissions

(a)Reports submitted up to 24 hours late lose 10% of the available marks.
(b)Reports submitted between 24 hours and one week late lose 30%.
(c)Reports more than one week late are not marked unless an extension was agreed in advance.

Academic integrity

You may discuss laboratory data with other students, but every report must be written individually. Generative tools may be used for spelling and grammar only, and their use must be declared1.

Suspected misconduct is referred to the faculty's academic integrity panel.

1 See the faculty guidance on the declaration of digital tools, section 4.2.
