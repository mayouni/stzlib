The Lighthouse Keepers of Saltmere

For more than a cen­tu­ry the light­house on Salt­mere Point was tend­ed by a sin­gle fam­i­ly. The first keep­er, a for­mer ship's car­pen­ter, ar­rived in 1871 with his wife and three chil­dren. He kept a de­tailed jour­nal of the weath­er, the pass­ing ves­sels and the con­di­tion of the great lamp. His de­scen­dants con­tin­ued the jour­nal with­out in­ter­rup­tion until the light was au­to­mat­ed in 1979.

The jour­nal is now held by the coun­ty ar­chive, where vol­un­teers are tran­scrib­ing it page by page. So far they have com­plet­ed the en­tries for the first forty years. The work is slow, be­cause the hand­writ­ing chang­es with every gen­er­a­tion and the ink has fad­ed in plac­es. Even so, the tran­scrip­tions al­ready re­veal a re­mark­able record of local life.

Storms are de­scribed with par­tic­u­lar care. In the win­ter of 1893 the keep­er noted that waves reached the gal­lery for three con­sec­u­tive nights. He spent those nights re­light­ing the lamp and wip­ing salt from the win­dows. Ship­wrecks are rarer than one might ex­pect, and most en­tries record or­di­nary days of main­te­nance and quiet ob­ser­va­tion.

The ar­chive plans an ex­hi­bi­tion for next sum­mer. Vis­i­tors will be able to read se­lect­ed pages along­side pho­to­graphs of the keep­ers and their fam­i­lies. A small com­mit­tee is also col­lect­ing rec­ol­lec­tions from peo­ple who vis­it­ed the light­house as chil­dren. Any­one with let­ters, pic­tures or sto­ries about Salt­mere Point is in­vit­ed to con­tact the ar­chive.

The or­gan­is­ers hope that the ex­hi­bi­tion will en­cour­age fur­ther re­search into the coast­al his­to­ry of the re­gion. They also in­tend to pub­lish the com­plete tran­scrip­tion on­line once the work is fin­ished.

Fund­ing for the pro­ject comes from a local trust and from the sale of a small il­lus­trat­ed guide. The guide de­scribes the light­house, its equip­ment and the daily rou­tine of the keep­ers. Copies are avail­able at the ar­chive and at the har­bour office.
