New tide gauge network goes live

Six new tide gauges along the Marrowby estuary began reporting in real time on 2 September. The gauges replace a network of float recorders that had been read by hand once a week since 1987. Readings are now published every five minutes and archived for public use.

The project was led by the instrumentation group, who spent most of the summer installing stilling wells at harbour walls and slipways. Two sites needed new concrete plinths after the spring storms undermined the old ones. Calibration against a portable reference gauge showed agreement within four millimetres at every site.

Readings are now published every five minutes.

Data users should note that the station codes have changed. A conversion table is available from the data office on request.

Archive digitisation reaches 1950

The archive team has now scanned every survey sheet held by the institute from 1950 to the present. Each sheet is stored as a high-resolution image together with a short catalogue record.

Older material, including the hand-drawn charts of the 1930s, needs conservation before it can be handled. A specialist paper conservator will visit in the new year to assess the worst affected volumes. Until then, researchers can view these charts only by appointment.

How to contribute

The newsletter welcomes short articles from staff, students and volunteers.

  1. Send a draft of no more than 400 words to the communications office.
  2. Attach photographs as separate files:
    1. at least 1,600 pixels wide;
    2. with the photographer named in the file name.
  3. Copy for the winter issue closes on 14 November.

Staff news

Dr Petra Lindqvist joins the sediment group as a postdoctoral researcher from 1 November. She comes from a fjord monitoring programme and will lead the new coring work.

After nineteen years in the workshop, Gerald Pyke retires at the end of October. Many of the instruments now in the field were built or rebuilt by his hands. A farewell tea will be held in the common room on his last Friday.
