1 Budget summary

The budget covers the drilling and equipment of three boreholes with solar pumps, storage tanks and tap stands, together with the training needed for communities to manage them. Equipment and works account for almost half of the total cost, which reflects the depth of the aquifer in the area.

Unit costs are based on three quotations obtained in September 2026 and include delivery to site. Indirect costs are charged at 7% of the direct costs requested from the donor, in line with the call for proposals.

3 Notes on selected budget lines

  • Line 1.3: each community contributes a monthly allowance for one of the two mobilisers.
  • Line 2.1: drilling depth is estimated at 85 metres; deeper boreholes would require a budget revision.
  • Line 2.3: communities provide sand, gravel and labour for the tank platforms, valued at USD 1,000 per village.
  • Line 4.3: the vehicle is owned by the Alliance, so only fuel and maintenance are charged.

4 Sustainability of running costs

After the project ends, each water committee will collect a small fee per jerrycan to pay the caretaker and replace worn parts. Solar pumps have no fuel costs, so the fee can stay below the price that households pay today to water vendors.

The Alliance will continue to visit each site twice a year for three years at its own cost.
