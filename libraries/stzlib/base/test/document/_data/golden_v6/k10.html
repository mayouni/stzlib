1. Executive summary

The programme remained broadly on track in the third quarter, with 31 of 40 planned boreholes completed and commissioned. Spending reached 68% of the annual budget against a planned 71%, mainly because the procurement of solar pumps slipped by six weeks. The overall status is amber because the delay to the pump contract now threatens the December handover of the Kilwa cluster.

Community uptake has been strong: tariff collection in the first commissioned villages averages 82% of the agreed monthly contribution, and no committee has yet reported a breakdown it could not repair from its own savings.

3. Progress against milestones

3.1 Water infrastructure

Drilling success rate rose to 86% after the geophysical survey method was changed in July. Nine boreholes remain to be drilled, all of them on the mainland side of the estuary, where rig access is not affected by the tides.

3.2 Community management

Water committees have been elected in 27 villages, and 19 of them have opened a maintenance savings account. Each committee has at least two women among its five officers, which is the minimum agreed in the community contracts.

5. Risks and issues

  • Late delivery of solar pumping systems (high)
    • Mitigation: interim diesel pumps hired for the four most affected sites.
    • Owner: procurement lead; next review on 15 November 2026.
  • Salinity above the guideline value in two boreholes (medium)
    • Mitigation: both boreholes sealed and replacement sites surveyed.
  • Rainy-season access to the island sites (medium)
    • Mitigation: materials pre-positioned before the end of October.

6. Priorities for next quarter

  1. Complete the remaining nine boreholes and their pump tests.
  2. Install and commission solar pumps at the first twelve sites.
  3. Elect the last three water committees and train all treasurers.
  4. Obtain donor sign-off of the baseline survey report.

7. Decisions requested from the steering committee

The steering committee is asked to approve a no-cost reallocation of 42,000 USD from training to infrastructure. It is also asked to note the revised handover date of 31 January 2027 for the Kilwa cluster.
